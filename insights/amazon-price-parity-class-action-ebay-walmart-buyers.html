Court Certifies Class of eBay and Walmart Buyers in Amazon Price-Parity Case

Atronia Innovations · 2026-11-08 · 5 min read

A long-running antitrust case over how Amazon's pricing rules affect prices on other marketplaces has moved forward. In a redacted order made public on November 2, 2026, a federal judge in Seattle certified a class of eBay and Walmart Marketplace shoppers who say Amazon's policies pushed sellers to keep prices higher everywhere. For sellers who list the same products on several channels, the case is worth understanding.

This is a summary of a court proceeding, not legal advice. Nothing has been decided on the merits.

What the court decided

In Frame-Wilson et al. v. Amazon.com, Inc. (No. 2:20-cv-00424, W.D. Wash.), U.S. District Judge John H. Chun granted in part the plaintiffs' motion for class certification, according to the court's redacted order (dated Nov 2, 2026). The certified class is:

All persons who, on or after March 19, 2016, made in the United States at least two purchases from a third-party seller on the Walmart online retail marketplace or at least four purchases from a third-party seller on eBay of new, physical products concurrently offered for sale by that same third-party seller on Amazon Marketplace and monitored by Amazon as part of its competitive price monitoring.

The court narrowed the plaintiffs' proposed definition by requiring that the product be offered by "that same" third-party seller on Amazon. Plaintiffs estimated the proposed class at "at least 10 million consumers," the order notes. VitalLaw reports the court rejected Amazon's argument that the class would be unmanageable.

What the plaintiffs allege

The plaintiffs claim Amazon maintains an "anti-discounting policy," a kind of platform most-favored-nation restraint that discourages third-party sellers from offering lower prices on competing marketplaces, in violation of Sections 1 and 2 of the Sherman Act. The order describes several policies they point to:

  • The Amazon Services Business Solutions Agreement and the policies it incorporates.
  • The Marketplace Fair Pricing Policy.
  • A 2021 clarification to the Seller Code of Conduct about off-Amazon incentives.
  • The SC-FOD tool, which plaintiffs say can remove Featured Offer (Buy Box) eligibility when a lower price is found elsewhere.

The judge wrote that plaintiffs' evidence "suggests that Amazon operates under a general policy of price parity via its contractual agreements with sellers." Amazon disputes this. According to Value Added Resource (Nov 4, 2026), Amazon argues SC-FOD compares prices only against a limited set of established retailers, that sellers can regain eligibility by lowering their Amazon price without changing prices elsewhere, and that its policies are lawful and benefit consumers. Value Added Resource also reports Amazon is seeking permission to appeal the certification to the Ninth Circuit.

What it means for sellers (our analysis)

No immediate change to Amazon's rules. Class certification doesn't change any Amazon policy today. Amazon's Featured Offer and pricing tools work as before while the case continues.

Multichannel pricing is under a spotlight. Between this case and other government actions over Amazon's pricing policies, how Amazon treats lower prices elsewhere is being examined closely. Keep records of how and why you set prices on each channel.

Know the trade-off you face today. If your price is lower on Walmart or your own site, your Amazon offer may lose Featured Offer eligibility. Many sellers manage this by aligning prices or by differentiating bundles per channel, but there is no single right answer.

Fees shape prices. Plaintiffs argue Amazon's fees feed into prices across channels. Whatever the outcome, knowing your all-in fee rate per channel, as in our Amazon vs Walmart referral fees comparison, is the basis for any pricing decision.

Checklist for multichannel sellers

  1. Document your pricing logic for each marketplace.
  2. Monitor Featured Offer status on your top Amazon ASINs.
  3. Calculate the all-in fee rate per channel before matching prices.
  4. Consider channel-specific bundles where price matching is uneconomic.
  5. Follow the case for any appeal decision or settlement.

法院认证 eBay 与 Walmart 买家集体：亚马逊价格一致反垄断案进展

Atronia Innovations · 2026-11-08 · 约 5 分钟阅读

一宗关于亚马逊定价规则如何影响其他平台价格的反垄断案有了新进展。2026 年 11 月 2 日公开的一份删节版裁定中，西雅图联邦法官认证了一个由 eBay 和 Walmart Marketplace 消费者组成的集体，他们指控亚马逊的政策迫使卖家在各渠道都维持较高价格。对在多个平台销售同款商品的卖家来说，这个案子值得了解。

本文是对诉讼程序的介绍，不构成法律意见，案件实体问题尚未判决。

法院裁定了什么

据法院删节版裁定（2026 年 11 月 2 日），在 Frame-Wilson 等诉 Amazon.com 案（华盛顿西区联邦法院，案号 2:20-cv-00424）中，美国地区法官 John H. Chun 部分批准了原告的集体认证申请。获认证的集体为：

自 2016 年 3 月 19 日起，在美国于 Walmart 平台向第三方卖家购买至少两次、或在 eBay 向第三方卖家购买至少四次的人士，所购为全新实物商品，且该商品同时由同一第三方卖家在亚马逊上销售，并受亚马逊竞争性价格监测。

法院收窄了原告提出的定义，要求商品须由"同一"第三方卖家在亚马逊上销售。裁定提到，原告估计拟议集体"至少 1,000 万名消费者"。据 VitalLaw报道，法院驳回了亚马逊认为该集体无法管理的主张。

原告指控什么

原告称亚马逊实行"反折扣政策"，即一种平台最惠国限制，阻止第三方卖家在其他平台提供更低价格，违反《谢尔曼法》第 1 条和第 2 条。裁定列出了原告援引的多项政策：

  • 亚马逊商业解决方案协议及其纳入的政策。
  • 平台公平定价政策（Marketplace Fair Pricing Policy）。
  • 2021 年对卖家行为准则中站外激励的澄清。
  • SC-FOD 工具：原告称当在别处发现更低价格时，它可能取消推荐报价（购物车）资格。

法官写道，原告的证据"表明亚马逊通过与卖家的合同协议实行一种普遍的价格一致政策"。亚马逊对此提出异议。据 Value Added Resource（2026 年 11 月 4 日），亚马逊称 SC-FOD 只与少数大型零售商比价，卖家只需降低亚马逊价格即可恢复资格而无需改动其他渠道价格，且其政策合法、有利于消费者。该文还报道，亚马逊正寻求向第九巡回上诉法院就集体认证提出上诉。

我们的解读

亚马逊规则暂无变化。集体认证不会改变亚马逊现行政策，案件进行期间，推荐报价和定价工具照常运作。

多渠道定价受到关注。加上政府针对亚马逊定价政策的其他行动，亚马逊如何对待其他渠道更低的价格正受到严格审视。保留好各渠道定价依据的记录。

认清当下的取舍。如果你在 Walmart 或独立站的价格更低，亚马逊报价可能失去推荐报价资格。很多卖家通过统一价格或按渠道做差异化组合来应对，没有唯一正确答案。

费用影响价格。原告认为亚马逊的费用传导到了各渠道价格上。无论结果如何，弄清各渠道的综合费率（可参考我们的亚马逊与 Walmart 佣金对比）都是定价决策的基础。

多渠道卖家清单

  1. 记录各平台的定价逻辑。
  2. 监控亚马逊主力 ASIN 的推荐报价状态。
  3. 跟价前先算清各渠道综合费率。
  4. 跟价不划算时，考虑按渠道做差异化组合装。
  5. 关注案件上诉或和解进展。
