Texas Moves to End Sales Tax on Marketplace Seller Fees

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-12 · 5 min read

Since October 1, 2025, Texas has treated the fees that online marketplaces charge sellers as a taxable "data processing service." On September 30, 2026, Texas Comptroller Don Huffines signed an executive order to reverse that. The catch: nothing changes until a formal rule amendment is adopted, so sellers who pay this tax should keep paying it for now. Here is what happened, the timeline, and what to do.

What changed in 2025

Texas has taxed data processing services since 1987. In 2025, under former Comptroller Glenn Hegar, the state amended its data processing rule (34 TAC Rule 3.330) to say that, effective October 1, 2025, "marketplace provider services may be included in taxable data processing services," according to Avalara (Oct 1, 2026). In practice, platforms began charging Texas sales tax on the fees they collect from sellers, such as commissions, listing and final value fees, and deducting it from seller payouts (Value Added Resource, Oct 10, 2026).

Critics called it a double tax: the shopper already pays sales tax on the item, and the seller then paid tax again on the marketplace's fee for the same sale.

What the new order does

  • Directs a rule change. The September 30, 2026 order instructs the Comptroller's office to amend Rule 3.330 so that fees charged to sellers for listing, offering or facilitating sales are not taxable data processing (Value Added Resource; Foley & Lardner, Oct 5, 2026).
  • Covers many platforms. Foley and Avalara list e-commerce marketplaces alongside food and grocery delivery, ride-hailing, short-term lodging, vehicle sharing, pet care and errand services. EcomCrew notes that for e-commerce sellers this includes seller fees on Amazon, eBay and Etsy.
  • Is not a full repeal. According to EcomCrew (Oct 6, 2026), computer-based data storage, web hosting and website maintenance remain taxable.
  • Comptroller's view. Huffines said: "The transaction fees charged by the marketplace providers are commissions, not data processing services."

The Comptroller has cautioned that the final rule may differ from the proposal. None of the sources we reviewed say whether tax already paid will be refunded or whether the change will apply retroactively.

How much money is involved

Texas's state sales tax rate is 6.25%, and local jurisdictions can add up to 2%, for a maximum combined rate of 8.25%, according to the Texas Comptroller. Avalara notes that 20% of the charge for data processing services is exempt, so the tax applies to 80% of the fee. A worked example for a seller paying $10,000 in marketplace fees on Texas-taxable activity:

ScenarioTaxable share of feesRateTax
State rate only$8,0006.25%$500
Maximum combined rate$8,0008.25%$660
If the amendment is adopted as proposed——$0 on marketplace fees

Calculation: $10,000 × 80% × rate. Actual amounts depend on how each platform calculates and itemizes the tax and on your local rate; check your fee invoices and settlement reports.

What it means for sellers (our analysis)

Who is affected. Huffines framed the tax as putting Texas sellers at a "disadvantage against out-of-state competitors who don't pay it," so sellers based in Texas are the clearest group. If you are not sure whether platforms charge you this tax, look for Texas tax lines on your fee invoices or settlement reports.

Don't stop paying early. EcomCrew warns that a seller who stops accruing the tax based only on the announcement is exposed for the gap period. Platforms that collect it will keep doing so until the rule changes.

Keep records for a possible refund. Save invoices showing the tax charged since October 2025. If a refund route opens, you will need them.

Comment if it matters to you. Once the proposal is published, anyone can submit comments during the 30-day period.

The bigger lesson: state taxes can change how much you keep from a sale, even when the marketplace collects the sales tax for you. Our new US sales tax guide for marketplace and DTC sellers explains the basics.

德州拟取消对平台卖家费用征收的销售税

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-12 · 约 5 分钟阅读

自 2025 年 10 月 1 日起，德州把电商平台向卖家收取的费用当作应税的“数据处理服务”征税。2026 年 9 月 30 日，德州财政长官（Comptroller）Don Huffines 签署行政令，要求撤销这一做法。但要注意：在正式规则修订通过之前什么都不会变，目前在交这笔税的卖家还得继续交。下面梳理来龙去脉、时间表和应对建议。

2025 年发生了什么

德州自 1987 年起对数据处理服务征税。2025 年，在前任财政长官 Glenn Hegar 任内，德州修订了数据处理规则（34 TAC Rule 3.330）。据 Avalara（2026 年 10 月 1 日）报道，新规写明自 2025 年 10 月 1 日起，“平台提供方服务可计入应税数据处理服务”。实际操作中，平台开始对向卖家收取的佣金、刊登费、成交费等加收德州销售税，并从卖家回款中扣除（Value Added Resource，2026 年 10 月 10 日）。

批评者认为这是重复征税：消费者买东西已经交了销售税，卖家又要为同一笔交易的平台费再交一次。

新行政令做了什么

  • 启动规则修订。9 月 30 日的行政令要求修订 Rule 3.330，明确向卖家收取的刊登、销售或促成交易的费用不属于应税数据处理（Value Added Resource；Foley & Lardner 律所，2026 年 10 月 5 日）。
  • 覆盖多类平台。Foley 和 Avalara 列出的范围包括电商平台、外卖与生鲜配送、网约车、短租、汽车共享、宠物服务和跑腿服务。EcomCrew 指出，对电商卖家而言包括亚马逊、eBay、Etsy 的卖家费用。
  • 不是全面废除。据 EcomCrew（2026 年 10 月 6 日），数据存储、网站托管和网站维护仍需缴税。
  • 财政长官的表态。Huffines 表示：“平台收取的交易费是佣金，不是数据处理服务。”

涉及多少钱

据德州财政长官办公室，德州州销售税率为 6.25%，地方最多可再加 2%，合计最高 8.25%。Avalara 指出，数据处理服务费用有 20% 免税，即按费用的 80% 计税。以一位在德州应税业务上支付 1 万美元平台费的卖家为例：

情形应税费用税率税额
仅州税$8,0006.25%$500
最高合计税率$8,0008.25%$660
若修订按草案通过——平台费免税（$0）

计算方法：$10,000 × 80% × 税率。实际金额取决于各平台的计算和列示方式及你所在地的税率，请以费用发票和结算报告为准。

我们的解读

谁受影响。Huffines 称这笔税让德州卖家“相比不用交税的州外竞争者处于劣势”，因此最明确受影响的是德州本地卖家。如果不确定自己是否被扣了这笔税，可在平台费用发票或结算报告里查找德州税费项目。

不要提前停缴。EcomCrew 提醒，仅凭公告就停止计提，过渡期内会有风险。在规则正式修改前，代扣的平台会继续代扣。

保留凭证以备退税。保存 2025 年 10 月以来显示该税项的发票，万一开放退税渠道会用得上。

需要的话可以提交意见。草案发布后的 30 天内，任何人都可以提交意见。

更重要的启示是：即使平台替你代收销售税，州税政策依然会影响你最终到手的钱。可阅读我们新发布的《美国销售税指南：平台卖家与独立站卖家》了解基础知识。
