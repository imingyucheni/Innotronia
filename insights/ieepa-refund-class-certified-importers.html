Court Certifies IEEPA Tariff Refund Class: What Importers Should Do

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-24 · 5 min read

Importers that never claimed their IEEPA tariff refunds may now have a second route. On October 8, 2026, the US Court of International Trade (CIT) certified a nationwide class of importers in a refund case, opening the door to relief for companies that did not sue on their own. It is not a refund cheque yet, but it matters for small importers and brands that bought on DDP terms and were unsure where they stood.

This is a follow-up to our earlier article on CAPE Phase 3, which covered CBP's refund process (CAPE). Nothing here is legal advice; talk to your customs broker or trade counsel about your own entries.

What the court decided

  • A certified class. On October 8, 2026, Senior Judge Richard K. Eaton certified a class in Freestyle World, Inc. v. United States (No. 26-01088), according to Thompson Hine (Oct 9, 2026) and Supply Chain Dive (Oct 9, 2026).
  • Who is in it. Importers of record that paid tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) between February 1, 2025 and February 19, 2026. Companies that filed their own lawsuits are excluded, per Mohawk Global (Oct 12, 2026) and Thompson Hine.
  • What it asks for. The class seeks an order directing CBP to liquidate or reliquidate entries without IEEPA duties and refund them. Certification itself does not order any payment yet.
  • Why the court did it. The order notes that "some importers of record have not taken advantage of CAPE" and that "it is believed that these are overwhelmingly small importers," Supply Chain Dive reports.

The numbers behind the case

According to the court order as reported by Supply Chain Dive, roughly $166 billion was collected under the IEEPA tariffs, and more than 4,000 importers had filed individual lawsuits. CBP has refunded about $126 billion including interest through CAPE as of early October, according to Mohawk Global and Thompson Hine citing CBP data (Supply Chain Dive put the figure at more than $100 billion). The court estimated that about $30 billion would stay with the government if there were no class relief.

What is still uncertain

  • No automatic money. As trade lawyer James Kim of ArentFox Schiff put it (quoted by Supply Chain Dive), "The decision doesn't immediately expand refund eligibility or direct payment."
  • Appeals. The government opposed certification, and the class order can be appealed. The Justice Department is also appealing a separate ruling on entries that were already finally liquidated, per Thompson Hine.
  • Timing. The court held a conference on October 13 and asked for scheduling proposals by October 22, so the next steps are still being set.
  • Deadlines. Some trade advisers suggest that importers with large refunds may still prefer to file their own case, and at least one flags possible filing deadlines in early 2027. Ask counsel how this applies to your entries.

What it means for sellers (our analysis)

Only the importer of record benefits. If your Chinese factory or a freight forwarder imported on DDP terms, the refund belongs to whoever is named as importer of record on the entry, not to you. Ask your supplier or forwarder whether they claimed through CAPE or sued, and whether your price agreement says anything about refunds.

Small importers gain the most. Brands that imported a few containers and never filed in CAPE or court are exactly who the court had in mind. You may be a class member without doing anything, but you still need your entry records ready.

Don't stop using CAPE. If your entries qualify for CAPE, that is still the fastest route. The class mostly helps those who missed it or whose entries don't fit.

Checklist for importers

  1. Pull an ACE report of all entries with IEEPA duties paid between Feb 1, 2025 and Feb 19, 2026.
  2. Check which entries were already refunded through CAPE.
  3. Confirm whether your company (or your broker on your behalf) filed a separate lawsuit, since that excludes you from the class.
  4. For DDP purchases, ask the importer of record how refunds will be handled.
  5. Talk to your customs broker or trade lawyer about filing your own action if your refund is large.
  6. Watch for court updates and any appeal.

美国法院认证 IEEPA 关税退款集体诉讼：进口商该怎么做

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-24 · 约 5 分钟阅读

没有申领 IEEPA 关税退款的进口商，现在可能多了一条路。2026 年 10 月 8 日，美国国际贸易法院（CIT）在一宗退税案中认证了全国性的进口商集体，意味着没有单独起诉的企业也有机会获得救济。这还不是退款到账，但对小进口商、以及以 DDP 条款采购、搞不清自己处境的品牌来说很关键。

本文是此前关于 CAPE 第三阶段的文章的后续，那篇介绍了 CBP 的退款流程（CAPE）。以下内容不构成法律意见，具体报关单请咨询你的报关行或贸易律师。

法院裁定了什么

  • 认证集体诉讼。据 Thompson Hine（2026 年 10 月 9 日）和 Supply Chain Dive（2026 年 10 月 9 日）报道，资深法官 Richard K. Eaton 于 10 月 8 日在 Freestyle World, Inc. v. United States（案号 26-01088）中认证了集体。
  • 谁在集体内。在 2025 年 2 月 1 日至 2026 年 2 月 19 日期间缴纳过 IEEPA（《国际紧急经济权力法》）关税的进口记录人（importer of record）。已自行起诉的企业不在集体内，据 Mohawk Global（2026 年 10 月 12 日）及 Thompson Hine。
  • 诉求是什么。集体要求法院命令 CBP 在不含 IEEPA 关税的情况下清算或重新清算报关单并退款。认证本身目前并不下令付款。
  • 法院的理由。据 Supply Chain Dive 报道，裁定指出"部分进口记录人没有使用 CAPE"，并且"据信这些绝大多数是小型进口商"。

案件背后的数字

据 Supply Chain Dive 援引法院裁定，IEEPA 关税共征收约 1,660 亿美元，已有超过 4,000 家进口商单独起诉。据 Mohawk Global 和 Thompson Hine 引用 CBP 数据，截至 10 月初 CAPE 已退还约 1,260 亿美元（含利息）；Supply Chain Dive 的说法是超过 1,000 亿美元。法院估计，如果没有集体救济，约 300 亿美元将留在政府手中。

仍不确定的地方

  • 不会自动退钱。ArentFox Schiff 律师 James Kim 对 Supply Chain Dive 表示，这一裁定"并不会立即扩大退款资格或直接付款"。
  • 上诉。政府反对认证，集体裁定可被上诉。据 Thompson Hine，司法部还在就另一项关于已最终清算报关单的裁定上诉。
  • 时间表。法院 10 月 13 日召开会议，要求各方在 10 月 22 日前提交时间安排建议，后续步骤仍在确定中。
  • 期限。有贸易顾问建议，退款金额大的进口商可能仍倾向于自行起诉；也有从业者提醒 2027 年初可能存在起诉期限。请咨询律师你的报关单如何适用。

我们的解读

只有进口记录人能拿到退款。如果是国内工厂或货代以 DDP 方式清关，退款归报关单上的进口记录人，而不是你。问清供应商或货代是否已走 CAPE 或起诉，以及双方的价格协议对退款有没有约定。

小进口商受益最大。只进口过几个柜、既没走 CAPE 也没起诉的品牌，正是法院考虑的对象。你可能无需任何操作就是集体成员，但报关记录要提前准备好。

能走 CAPE 的继续走 CAPE。符合条件的报关单，CAPE 仍是最快的路径。集体诉讼主要帮助错过 CAPE 或报关单不符合条件的企业。

进口商行动清单

  1. 从 ACE 导出 2025 年 2 月 1 日至 2026 年 2 月 19 日期间所有缴纳过 IEEPA 关税的报关单。
  2. 核对哪些报关单已通过 CAPE 退款。
  3. 确认公司（或报关行代你）是否单独起诉过——起诉过就不在集体内。
  4. DDP 采购的，向进口记录人确认退款如何处理。
  5. 退款金额较大的，和报关行或贸易律师讨论是否自行起诉。
  6. 持续关注法院进展和上诉情况。
