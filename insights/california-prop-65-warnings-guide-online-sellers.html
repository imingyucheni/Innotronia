California Prop 65 for Online Sellers: Who Must Warn and How

Atronia Innovations · 2026-11-14 · 6 min read

If you sell consumer products that reach California buyers, Proposition 65 can apply to you even if your company is based in China or another state. A missing warning can lead to a "60-day notice," a settlement demand and civil penalties. This guide explains who must warn, how online warnings work, and the dates and numbers that matter, based on official California guidance.

What Prop 65 requires

According to the OEHHA Frequently Asked Questions for Businesses, businesses must provide a "clear and reasonable" warning before knowingly and intentionally exposing anyone to a chemical on the Proposition 65 list, unless they can show the exposure will not pose a significant risk of cancer or is significantly below levels observed to cause birth defects or other reproductive harm. The list covers chemicals known to cause cancer or reproductive harm, and businesses decide for themselves whether their products need a warning.

Two points sellers often miss:

  • Out-of-state online sellers are not exempt. The FAQ says Prop 65 "applies to all businesses with 10 or more employees doing business in California," including out-of-state internet retailers.
  • Small businesses may still be affected. Companies with fewer than 10 employees are exempt from the warning rules, but OEHHA notes they may still have contractual or indemnification obligations, for example to a retailer or marketplace.

Who has to provide the warning

OEHHA's regulations place primary responsibility on manufacturers, producers, packagers, importers, suppliers and distributors. Under 27 CCR 25600.2, they must either put the warning on the product label or labeling, or send written notice and warning materials to the retailer's authorized agent and get an acknowledgment. Retail sellers are responsible for placing and maintaining the warning materials they receive. For a Chinese factory or brand selling through US retailers or marketplaces, that usually means you are expected to provide the warning.

How online warnings work

For internet sales, the FAQ says a product needs both a warning on or with the product and a warning online. The website must display the warning on the product display page, include a clearly marked hyperlink using the word "WARNING," or otherwise prominently display it "prior to completing the purchase." OEHHA gives the example of a pop-up shown when a buyer enters a California ZIP code. If the label uses a short-form warning, the website may use the same short-form content.

Short-form warnings: the 2028 change

The short-form warning is a shorter label version with the yellow warning symbol. According to OEHHA, products manufactured and labeled before January 1, 2028 can use the prior short-form or the new one. From January 1, 2028, the new language is required to keep safe harbor protection, and it must name at least one listed chemical for each health effect (cancer or reproductive harm). A short-form label warning cannot be smaller than 6-point type.

If you are printing packaging now for products that will still be manufactured in 2028, switching to the new short-form content today avoids a second packaging change.

Practical checklist

  1. Check your materials (plastics, coatings, metals, dyes) against the current Prop 65 list with your supplier or a testing lab.
  2. Decide: reformulate to avoid the chemical, or warn. The Attorney General's FAQ confirms reformulating is an option.
  3. If you warn, use the safe harbor content and add the warning to both the label and every online listing that can ship to California.
  4. If you sell through retailers, send written notice and warning materials, get an acknowledgment, and renew it at least once a year.
  5. Keep records of test reports, notices and listing screenshots.
  6. Align Prop 65 work with your other compliance steps: country-of-origin marking, CPSC product certificates and liability insurance.
  7. If you receive a 60-day notice, act fast and consult a California attorney.

加州 65 号提案（Prop 65）卖家指南：谁要加警示、网上怎么标

Atronia Innovations · 2026-11-14 · 约 6 分钟阅读

只要你的消费品会卖给加州买家，即使公司在中国或美国其他州，也可能受加州 65 号提案（Prop 65）约束。缺少警示标签可能招来"60 天通知"、和解索赔和民事罚款。本文根据加州官方指引，讲清楚谁需要提供警示、网上销售怎么标、以及关键数字和期限。

Prop 65 要求什么

根据 OEHHA 企业常见问题解答，企业在明知且有意让任何人接触 Prop 65 清单上的化学物质之前，必须提供"清楚且合理"的警示；除非企业能证明该接触不会构成显著致癌风险，或明显低于导致出生缺陷或其他生殖危害的水平。清单包括已知致癌或导致生殖危害的化学物质，是否需要警示由企业自行判断。

卖家常忽略的两点：

  • 州外网店不豁免。FAQ 明确：Prop 65 适用于所有在加州开展业务、员工 10 人及以上的企业，包括州外的网络零售商。
  • 小企业也可能受影响。员工不足 10 人的企业不受警示规定约束，但 OEHHA 提醒，它们仍可能对零售商或平台负有合同或赔偿义务。

谁负责提供警示

OEHHA 的规定把主要责任放在制造商、生产商、包装商、进口商、供应商和分销商身上。根据 27 CCR 25600.2，他们要么在产品标签或说明材料上标注警示，要么向零售商的授权代理发送书面通知和警示材料，并取得确认回执。零售商负责摆放和维护收到的警示材料。对通过美国零售商或平台销售的中国工厂和品牌来说，这通常意味着警示责任在你。

网上销售怎么警示

FAQ 说明，网上销售的产品既要在产品上（或随产品）提供警示，也要在网上提供警示。网站必须在商品详情页展示警示，或用带"WARNING"字样的明显超链接指向警示内容，或以其他方式在"完成购买之前"醒目地展示。OEHHA 举的例子是：买家输入加州邮编时弹出警示。如果标签使用简版警示，网站也可以使用同样的简版内容。

简版警示：2028 年的变化

简版警示（short-form）是带黄色警示符号的简短标签。根据 OEHHA，2028 年 1 月 1 日前生产并贴标的产品，可以使用旧版或新版简版警示；从 2028 年 1 月 1 日起，必须使用新版内容才能享受"安全港"保护，且需针对每一种健康影响（致癌或生殖危害）至少写出一种清单化学物质的名称。标签上的简版警示字号不得小于 6 磅。

如果你现在印制的包装要用到 2028 年以后生产的产品上，建议直接换成新版简版警示，避免两次改包装。

实操清单

  1. 与供应商或检测机构一起，把产品材料（塑料、涂层、金属、染料等）与最新 Prop 65 清单比对。
  2. 二选一：改配方避开该化学物质，或者提供警示。加州总检察长 FAQ 确认改配方是可行选项。
  3. 如需警示，使用安全港内容，并在产品标签和所有可能发往加州的网上 listing 上同时添加。
  4. 通过零售商销售的，发送书面通知和警示材料、取得回执，并至少每年更新一次。
  5. 保存检测报告、通知记录和 listing 截图。
  6. 把 Prop 65 与其他合规工作一起做：原产地标识、CPSC 产品合格证书 和 产品责任险。
  7. 收到 60 天通知要尽快处理，并咨询加州律师。
