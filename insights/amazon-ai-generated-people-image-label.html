Amazon AI Image Rules: How to Label AI-Generated People in Listings

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-04 · 5 min read

AI tools make it cheap to create lifestyle photos with models, and many sellers now use them for main images, A+ Content and videos. Since July 2026, Amazon has required sellers to label images and videos that contain photorealistic AI-generated people. Getting it wrong can cost you images or listing visibility. Here is what the rule covers, how to add the label, and how to stay safe.

What the rule requires

According to eWeek and GeekSeller, Amazon notified sellers in late July 2026 that:

  • Product images, lifestyle images, product videos and A+ Content that contain photorealistic AI-generated people must be tagged before upload.
  • The tag is the keyword contains-synthetic-performer, added to the dc:subject (XMP) metadata field with an IPTC-compatible editor.
  • Amazon will show a disclosure on product listings where applicable, and can remove non-compliant images or suppress listings.

CNBC and Forbes also reported the change, linking it to a New York law on synthetic performers in advertising.

The law behind it

eWeek reports that the policy follows New York General Business Law Section 396-b, which took effect on June 9, 2026 and requires businesses that create ads with "synthetic performers" to disclose it. Penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each later violation. Other states are considering similar rules, so expect more disclosure requirements over time. This article is general information, not legal advice.

Does my image need the label?

Image contentLabel needed?
A photorealistic person created entirely by AIYes
A real model, retouched or edited with AI toolsNo (per Amazon's guidance as reported)
Characters from films, TV or video gamesNo
Cartoon or clearly non-photorealistic peopleNo
Product only, no peopleNo
AI background with a real person composited inNot addressed in the reports we reviewed; check Amazon's latest wording in Seller Central before uploading

Source: Amazon's seller notice as reported by eWeek and GeekSeller, July 2026. When an image is borderline, check the current Seller Central guidance or choose a real model.

How to add the metadata

  1. Keep the original file. Some marketplaces and image tools strip metadata, so tag the final export you will upload.
  2. Use an IPTC/XMP-capable editor. Desktop photo software with a "keywords" or "File Info" panel, or the free command-line tool ExifTool, can write XMP keywords.
  3. Add the exact keyword contains-synthetic-performer to the dc:subject (keywords) field. Spelling matters.
  4. Check that it saved by reopening the file's metadata before upload.
  5. Apply it to videos and A+ modules too, not only main images.
  6. Keep a log of which ASINs use AI-generated people, which files were tagged and when.
Tip: Agencies and freelancers often deliver AI-generated lifestyle images without saying so. Ask every creative supplier to state in writing whether people in the images are real or AI-generated.

Should you still use AI-generated models?

AI imagery can cut cost and speed up testing, but think about the trade-offs:

  • Disclosure may affect trust. Some shoppers react differently to a visible AI label. Test conversion with and without AI models.
  • Accuracy rules still apply. AI images must still show the real product accurately; misleading images break listing policies regardless of labels.
  • Other channels. If you reuse the same images on Walmart, TikTok Shop or your own site, check each platform's rules and state laws for ads.

Audit checklist for existing listings

  1. List all ASINs with lifestyle images, videos or A+ modules that show people.
  2. Confirm with your designer or agency which ones were AI-generated.
  3. Re-export those files with the metadata tag and re-upload.
  4. Replace any images where you cannot confirm the source.
  5. Add the check to your standard listing launch process, and repeat the audit whenever you refresh creative for a new season.

亚马逊 AI 图片新规：如何标注 AI 生成的人物

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-04 · 约 5 分钟阅读

AI 工具让制作带模特的场景图变得很便宜，很多卖家已经把它用在主图、A+ 页面和视频里。自 2026 年 7 月起，亚马逊要求卖家对包含逼真 AI 生成人物的图片和视频进行标注。处理不当可能导致图片被删或 Listing 曝光受限。下面讲清楚规则覆盖范围、如何添加标注，以及如何规避风险。

规则要求什么

根据 eWeek 和 GeekSeller 的报道，亚马逊在 2026 年 7 月下旬通知卖家：

  • 包含逼真 AI 生成人物的商品图、场景图、商品视频和 A+ 内容，上传前必须加标注；
  • 标注方式是用支持 IPTC 的编辑工具，在 dc:subject（XMP）元数据字段中加入关键词 contains-synthetic-performer；
  • 亚马逊会在相应 Listing 上展示提示，并可能删除不合规图片或限制 Listing 展示。

CNBC 和 福布斯 也报道了这一变化，并指出它与纽约州关于广告中“合成表演者”的法律有关。

背后的法律

据 eWeek 报道，该政策源于纽约州《一般商业法》第 396-b 条，2026 年 6 月 9 日生效，要求制作含“合成表演者”广告的商家进行披露。违规罚款为首次 1,000 美元，此后每次 5,000 美元。其他州也在考虑类似规定，未来披露要求可能更多。本文仅为一般信息，不构成法律意见。

我的图片需要标注吗？

图片内容是否需要标注
完全由 AI 生成的逼真人物需要
真实模特，用 AI 工具修图或编辑不需要（据报道的亚马逊说明）
电影、电视、游戏角色不需要
卡通或明显非写实的人物不需要
只有产品，没有人物不需要
AI 生成背景 + 合成的真人我们查阅的报道未明确说明，上传前请核对卖家后台的最新表述

来源：eWeek 与 GeekSeller 报道的亚马逊卖家通知，2026 年 7 月。拿不准的图片，请查看卖家后台最新说明，或改用真人模特。

如何添加元数据

  1. 保留原始文件：部分平台和图片工具会清除元数据，要在最终上传的导出文件上打标。
  2. 使用支持 IPTC/XMP 的编辑器：带“关键词”或“文件简介”面板的桌面图片软件，或免费命令行工具 ExifTool，都可以写入 XMP 关键词。
  3. 准确填写关键词 contains-synthetic-performer 到 dc:subject（关键词）字段，拼写必须一致。
  4. 确认已保存：上传前重新打开文件的元数据检查一遍。
  5. 视频和 A+ 模块也要标注，不只是主图。
  6. 做好记录：哪些 ASIN 使用了 AI 人物、哪些文件已打标、何时完成。
小提示：设计公司和自由职业者交付的场景图常常是 AI 生成的却没有说明。请所有美工供应商书面确认图片中的人物是真人还是 AI 生成。

还要不要用 AI 模特？

AI 图片能降低成本、加快测图，但要权衡：

  • 披露可能影响信任：部分买家看到 AI 标识后反应不同，建议对比测试有无 AI 模特的转化率。
  • 准确性要求不变：AI 图片仍必须如实展示产品，误导性图片无论是否标注都违反 Listing 政策。
  • 其他渠道：如果同一套图片还用在沃尔玛、TikTok Shop 或独立站，要分别核对各平台规则和各州广告法规。

现有 Listing 自查清单

  1. 列出所有含人物的场景图、视频或 A+ 模块的 ASIN。
  2. 向设计师或服务商确认哪些是 AI 生成的。
  3. 重新导出并加上元数据标注后再上传。
  4. 无法确认来源的图片直接替换。
  5. 把这项检查加入标准上新流程，每次换季更新素材时再查一遍。
