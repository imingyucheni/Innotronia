Amazon Holiday Returns: A Seller's Guide to the Extended Window

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-09 · 6 min read

Every year, the money you make in November and December is not final until the end of January. Amazon extends its return window for holiday purchases, and online orders already come back more often than store purchases. This guide explains how the extended holiday returns policy has worked, what the data says about return rates, and a practical plan to protect your Q4 margin.

How Amazon's extended holiday returns work

Amazon's normal policy lets customers return most items within 30 days of delivery. For the holidays, Amazon has extended that window for several years in a row. For the 2025 season, Amazon's notice to sellers said items bought between November 1 and December 31, 2025 were returnable through January 31, 2026, with Apple-brand products returnable through January 15, 2026, as reported by Value Added Resource (updated Oct 14, 2025).

Three details matter for sellers:

  • It covers FBA and seller-fulfilled orders. Amazon's notice said the extended window applies to seller-fulfilled, FBA and Amazon retail orders.
  • Only the deadline changes. Amazon stated that "returns eligibility for all orders remains the same." Items that are not returnable (for example, Final Sale, perishable or customized items) stay non-returnable.
  • The start date has moved. In 2022, Amazon announced an October 7 start that would have caught October sale purchases, then changed course after seller pushback, according to Value Added Resource; in 2023, 2024 and 2025, the window started on November 1.

What the data says about return rates

The National Retail Federation and Happy Returns (a UPS company) surveyed 358 e-commerce professionals at large US merchants for their 2025 Retail Returns Landscape (Oct 15, 2025). Retailers estimated that 15.8% of 2025 sales would be returned, totaling $849.9 billion, compared with 16.9% and $890 billion the year before. Online orders are higher, at an estimated 19.3%, and retailers expected about 17% of holiday sales to come back.

The same report found that 9% of all returns are fraudulent, and that 82% of consumers call free returns a major consideration when buying. In other words, a smooth return process helps sales, but holiday returns are large enough that sellers need a plan for them.

Plan your holiday returns in five steps

1. Budget for returns in your Q4 pricing

Use your own return rate by SKU from last November–January, not just the national average. Build it into your deal prices: a 20% discount on a product with a 15% return rate leaves much less margin than the sticker suggests. Remember that sales from November and December may be reversed in January, so do not spend Q4 profit before February.

2. Prepare for FBA returns

For FBA orders, Amazon receives the return and decides whether the unit is sellable. Review your FBA customer returns report every week in January, and check that returned units that should be resaleable go back into stock. If a return was refunded but the item never came back, or came back damaged by the carrier or warehouse, look at whether you are eligible for a reimbursement under Amazon's FBA policies.

3. Get seller-fulfilled returns ready

If you ship orders yourself, make sure your return address and return settings in Seller Central are correct before November, and that someone will process return requests over the holidays. Decide in advance how you will handle low-value items where return shipping costs more than the product.

4. Reduce avoidable returns

Many returns come from wrong expectations. Before the holiday traffic arrives, check size charts, dimensions, compatibility notes and the main images. Read last season's return reasons and fix the top two causes in your listing.

5. Watch for fraud and abuse

Given the 9% fraud estimate, track returns that come back empty, swapped or used. Keep photos of high-value items before shipping and file claims quickly with evidence.

Holiday returns checklist

  1. Pull last year's return rate by SKU for November–January.
  2. Add expected returns to your deal and ad budget calculations.
  3. Confirm return address, settings and holiday staffing for seller-fulfilled orders.
  4. Fix the top return reasons in listings before Black Friday.
  5. Check Amazon's 2026 holiday return dates once announced.
  6. Review FBA returns and reimbursement eligibility weekly in January.

亚马逊假日延长退货：卖家应对指南

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-09 · 约 6 分钟阅读

每年 11、12 月赚的钱，要到 1 月底才真正落袋。亚马逊会为假日季订单延长退货期，而线上订单本来退货率就高于线下。本文梳理亚马逊“假日延长退货”政策的历年规则、退货率数据，以及一套保护 Q4 利润的实操方案。

亚马逊假日延长退货怎么运作

亚马逊平时的政策是：大多数商品在送达后 30 天内可退。假日季这一窗口已连续多年延长。据 Value Added Resource（2025 年 10 月 14 日更新）报道，亚马逊 2025 年给卖家的通知显示：2025 年 11 月 1 日至 12 月 31 日购买的商品，可在 2026 年 1 月 31 日前退货；苹果品牌商品截止 2026 年 1 月 15 日。

卖家需要注意三点：

  • FBA 和自发货（FBM）订单都适用。亚马逊通知明确，延长窗口适用于卖家自配送、FBA 和亚马逊自营订单。
  • 只延长期限，不改变可退范围。亚马逊表示“所有订单的退货资格保持不变”。原本不可退的商品（如 Final Sale、生鲜、定制商品）依然不可退。
  • 起始日期有过变化。据 Value Added Resource 报道，2022 年亚马逊曾宣布从 10 月 7 日开始（会覆盖秋季大促订单），后因卖家反对而调整；2023、2024、2025 年都从 11 月 1 日开始。

退货率数据怎么说

美国零售联合会（NRF）与 Happy Returns（UPS 旗下）在《2025 零售退货报告》（2025 年 10 月 15 日）中调研了 358 位美国大型零售商的电商负责人。零售商预计 2025 年销售额的 15.8% 会被退回，合计 8,499 亿美元；上一年为 16.9%、8,900 亿美元。线上订单退货率更高，预计达 19.3%；假日季销售预计约 17% 会被退回。

报告还指出，9% 的退货属于欺诈性退货；82% 的消费者把免费退货视为购物时的重要考量。也就是说，顺畅的退货体验有助于转化，但假日季退货量大到必须提前规划。

五步做好假日退货规划

1. 把退货成本算进 Q4 定价

用你自己去年 11 月至次年 1 月按 SKU 统计的退货率，而不只是行业均值，把它算进秒杀和折扣价。一个退货率 15% 的产品打 8 折，实际剩下的毛利比账面低得多。11、12 月的销售可能在 1 月被退款冲回，所以 2 月前别急着把 Q4 利润花掉。

2. 做好 FBA 退货跟踪

FBA 订单由亚马逊接收退货并判定是否可售。1 月份每周查看 FBA 买家退货报告，确认应可再售的商品已回到可售库存。若出现已退款但商品未退回、或因承运商/仓库原因损坏的情况，按亚马逊 FBA 政策核对是否符合赔偿条件。

3. 自发货卖家提前准备

自发货卖家请在 11 月前确认卖家后台的退货地址和退货设置无误，并安排假期有人处理退货请求。提前决定低价商品（退货运费比货值还高）如何处理。

4. 减少可避免的退货

很多退货源于“与预期不符”。流量高峰前，检查尺码表、尺寸、兼容性说明和主图；翻看上一季的退货原因，先把排名前两位的问题在 Listing 中解决。

5. 防范欺诈与滥用

既然约 9% 的退货属于欺诈，就要留意空箱、调包、二手退回等情况。高价商品发货前留存照片，发现问题及时带证据提交索赔。

假日退货检查清单

  1. 导出去年 11 月至 1 月按 SKU 的退货率。
  2. 在秒杀和广告预算测算中加入预估退货。
  3. 自发货：确认退货地址、设置和假期人手。
  4. 黑五前修复 Listing 中的主要退货原因。
  5. 亚马逊公布 2026 年假日退货日期后及时核对。
  6. 1 月每周复查 FBA 退货与赔偿资格。
