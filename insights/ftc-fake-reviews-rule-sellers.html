The FTC Fake Reviews Rule: What Sellers Can and Can't Do

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-14 · 6 min read

Reviews sell products, which is exactly why regulators watch them closely. Since October 21, 2024, the US Federal Trade Commission's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule has banned fake reviews, paying for positive sentiment and several other tactics, with civil penalties for knowing violations. For brands and factories selling to US customers, this guide explains what the rule prohibits, what is still allowed, and how to build reviews safely.

The rule in brief

The FTC announced the final rule on August 14, 2024 after a 5-0 vote, and it went into effect on October 21, 2024. The rule (16 CFR Part 465) lets courts impose civil penalties for knowing violations. Under the FTC's penalty schedule in 16 CFR § 1.98, the maximum for violations under Section 5(m)(1)(A) of the FTC Act is $53,088 per violation for penalties assessed after January 17, 2025.

Penalties are counted per violation. When each fake review or each incentivized review can be a separate violation, the totals can grow quickly.

What the rule prohibits

SectionProhibited practiceWhat it looks like for sellers
§ 465.2Fake or false reviews and testimonialsReviews by people who don't exist (including AI-generated) or who misrepresent their experience; buying such reviews or posting them when you knew or should have known they were fake
§ 465.4Buying positive or negative reviewsOffering a refund, gift card or free product on the condition, stated or implied, that the review is positive (or that a competitor's is negative)
§ 465.5Undisclosed insider reviewsReviews by employees, managers or their relatives without clear disclosure of the connection
§ 465.6Company-controlled "independent" review sitesRunning a "best products" site that presents itself as independent while featuring your own brand
§ 465.7Review suppressionThreats or intimidation to remove negative reviews, or claiming displayed reviews represent all reviews when negatives were suppressed
§ 465.8Fake social media influenceBuying fake followers or views to look more influential for commercial purposes

What is still allowed

According to the FTC's Q&A, businesses can offer incentives for reviews as long as there is no "express or implied requirement" that the reviews express a particular sentiment. The FTC adds that failing to disclose incentives could still violate the FTC Act. Marketplace rules can be stricter than the FTC rule: Amazon, for example, does not allow sellers to offer incentives for reviews outside its own programs, so always follow the platform's current review policy as well.

Practices that generally fit both the law and marketplace rules:

  • Using the platform's own review tools, such as Amazon's "Request a Review" button or the Vine program for eligible brands.
  • Neutral follow-up messages that ask for honest feedback, without mentioning stars or offering anything.
  • Influencer and creator content with clear disclosure of free products or payment.
  • Replying to negative reviews politely and fixing the underlying issue.

Common risks for cross-border sellers

  • Insert cards. Cards that offer a gift or refund "for a 5-star review" break the rule's ban on buying positive sentiment and marketplace policy.
  • Review services. Agencies that promise a set number of reviews often rely on fake or paid reviews. Under the rule, you can be liable if you knew or should have known.
  • Staff and family reviews. Reviews from factory staff, sales teams or their relatives count as insider reviews.
  • Pressure on critics. Threatening a customer over a bad review can be treated as review suppression.
  • Bought followers. Buying followers or views for a brand or creator account to look bigger is covered by the rule.

Review compliance checklist

  1. Remove any insert card or message that links a reward to a review or to a star rating.
  2. Stop any service that "guarantees" reviews; keep records of who you hire.
  3. Tell employees and partners not to review your products, or to disclose the relationship where allowed.
  4. Require clear disclosures in creator and influencer agreements.
  5. Answer negative reviews with facts and solutions, never threats.
  6. Check each marketplace's current review policy before running any review campaign.

This article is general information, not legal advice.

FTC 虚假评论规则：卖家能做什么、不能做什么

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-14 · 约 6 分钟阅读

评论能卖货，所以监管机构盯得很紧。自 2024 年 10 月 21 日起，美国联邦贸易委员会（FTC）的《消费者评论与推荐规则》禁止虚假评论、花钱买好评等多种做法，明知故犯可被处以民事罚款。本文面向向美国消费者销售的品牌和工厂，讲清楚规则禁止什么、什么仍然允许，以及如何合规地积累评论。

规则要点

FTC 于 2024 年 8 月 14 日以 5:0 投票通过该最终规则，并于 2024 年 10 月 21 日生效。规则（16 CFR Part 465）允许法院对明知违规的行为处以民事罚款。根据 16 CFR § 1.98中 FTC 的罚款标准，2025 年 1 月 17 日之后裁定的、依据《FTC 法》第 5(m)(1)(A) 条的罚款，上限为每次违规 53,088 美元。

罚款按“每次违规”计算。如果每一条虚假评论或诱导评论都算一次违规，总额会迅速放大。

规则禁止什么

条款禁止行为对卖家来说是什么样子
§ 465.2虚假评论和推荐不存在的人（包括 AI 生成）或歪曲真实体验的评论；购买此类评论，或明知/应知是假的仍发布
§ 465.4花钱买好评或差评以明示或暗示要求好评（或给竞品差评）为条件，提供返现、礼品卡或免费产品
§ 465.5未披露的内部人员评论员工、管理人员或其亲属写评论却不清楚披露关系
§ 465.6公司控制的“独立”测评网站自建“好物推荐”网站，自称独立，却推荐自家品牌
§ 465.7压制评论用威胁、恐吓迫使删除差评，或在差评被压下的情况下宣称展示的是全部评论
§ 465.8虚假社交影响力为商业目的购买假粉丝、假播放量，让账号看起来更有影响力

来源：FTC 新闻稿（2024 年 8 月 14 日）及 FTC 规则问答。

什么仍然允许

根据 FTC 问答，企业可以为评论提供激励，前提是没有“明示或暗示”要求评论表达特定倾向。FTC 同时提醒，不披露激励仍可能违反《FTC 法》。平台规则可能比 FTC 更严格：例如亚马逊不允许卖家在其官方项目之外为评论提供任何激励，所以务必同时遵守平台最新的评论政策。

一般同时符合法律和平台规则的做法：

  • 使用平台自带的评论工具，例如亚马逊的“请求评论”（Request a Review）按钮，或符合条件的品牌使用 Vine 计划。
  • 发送中性的跟进消息，请买家给出真实反馈，不提星级、不提供任何回报。
  • 达人和网红内容清楚披露免费送样或付费合作。
  • 礼貌回复差评，并真正解决问题。

跨境卖家的常见风险

  • 好评返现卡。“五星好评返现/送礼”的卡片既违反规则中禁止购买好评的条款，也违反平台政策。
  • 刷评服务。承诺固定评论数量的服务商往往依赖虚假或付费评论。按规则，只要你“知道或应当知道”，就可能承担责任。
  • 员工和亲友评论。工厂员工、销售团队或其亲属的评论都属于内部人员评论。
  • 向差评买家施压。因差评威胁顾客，可能被认定为压制评论。
  • 买粉丝。为品牌号或达人号购买粉丝、播放量来“撑场面”，也在规则覆盖范围内。

评论合规清单

  1. 撤掉所有把回报与评论或星级挂钩的卡片和消息。
  2. 停用任何“保证评论数量”的服务，并留存合作方记录。
  3. 告知员工和合作伙伴不要评论自家产品，或在允许的情况下披露关系。
  4. 在达人和网红合作协议中要求清楚披露。
  5. 用事实和解决方案回复差评，绝不威胁。
  6. 做任何评论相关活动前，先核对各平台最新的评论政策。

本文为一般性信息，不构成法律意见。
