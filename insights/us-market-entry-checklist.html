Selling in the US: 8 Things to Prepare Before You Launch

Atronia Innovations · 2026-09-26 · 5 min read

Many factories arrive in the US with a great product and a strong price, only to stall because a listing is suppressed, a shipment is held at customs, or a competitor hijacks the brand. Almost all of these problems can be avoided with preparation. Here are eight things to have in place before your first US launch.

Requirements vary by product category, marketplace and state, and they change over time. Use this list as a starting point, and confirm details with official sources and qualified professionals (customs brokers, trademark attorneys, tax advisors).

1. Business entity and tax basics

Whether you need a US company depends on where and how you sell. Some marketplaces accept China-based businesses directly; others, or certain programs within them, may ask for a US entity, a US tax ID or a US bank account. Check each platform's current seller requirements before you set anything up.

On sales tax: most US states with a sales tax have marketplace facilitator laws, which generally make the marketplace responsible for collecting and remitting sales tax on orders placed through it. Sales through your own website are different and may create obligations in states where you reach certain sales thresholds. Income tax and entity structure are separate questions. This is not tax advice, so speak with a cross-border tax professional before deciding.

2. A US trademark

A registered or pending US trademark protects your brand name and is often the key to brand programs such as Amazon Brand Registry and similar brand tools on other marketplaces. Those programs typically unlock enhanced content, brand stores, better control over listing details, and tools for reporting infringement. Check each program's current eligibility rules.

Two practical points: file early, because examination by the USPTO can take many months; and note that applicants based outside the US are generally required to use a US-licensed attorney. Search for conflicting marks before you print packaging.

3. Product compliance and certifications

US rules are category-specific, and marketplaces may ask for documents before they let you sell. A few common examples (not a complete list):

  • Electronics: devices that emit radio frequency energy usually need FCC equipment authorization.
  • Children's products: items designed for children 12 and under generally need third-party testing by a CPSC-accepted lab and a Children's Product Certificate.
  • FDA-regulated items: food, cosmetics, medical devices and some related products have their own registration, labeling or listing rules.

Other categories may involve UL-type safety standards, state rules such as California Proposition 65 warnings, or restricted-product approvals. Confirm your category's requirements with a testing lab or compliance consultant.

4. Product liability insurance

If a product causes injury or damage in the US, the seller can face costly claims. Some marketplaces require sellers to carry product liability insurance once they reach certain sales levels, and large retailers often ask for it as well. Even when it is not required, it is worth pricing a policy early so the cost is built into your margins. Check the platform's current coverage and "additional insured" requirements.

5. Importing and logistics

Every shipment needs an importer of record (IOR), the party responsible for customs compliance, correct classification and paying duties. Marketplaces generally do not act as your IOR, so plan who will fill this role. A licensed customs broker can file entries and advise on HS codes, customs bonds and current duty rates, which have changed often in recent years.

After customs, you have several fulfillment options:

  • FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon) or WFS (Walmart Fulfillment Services): the marketplace stores, picks, packs and ships.
  • A US 3PL warehouse: one inventory pool serving several channels, including your own site.
  • Self-fulfillment with discounted labels: useful for smaller volumes or oversized items. Compare rates with our shipping calculator and see how to cut US shipping costs.

6. Listing content and localization

US shoppers compare quickly. Your listing should feel native:

  • Use US units (inches, pounds, fluid ounces, °F) and US plug and voltage specs where relevant.
  • Write natural American English: clear benefit-led titles and bullet points, not machine translation.
  • Follow each marketplace's image rules (for example, clean main images) and add lifestyle images showing US-style settings.
  • Research the keywords US buyers actually search, which may differ from your internal product names.

7. Advertising budget and launch plan

New listings rarely sell on their own. Plan a launch budget for sponsored ads, samples and early review-generation programs that comply with platform rules. Expect to spend more in the first weeks while the algorithm learns and you test keywords, and set a target ACoS or TACoS so you know when to scale or cut back. Time your launch around inventory arrival, and avoid starting just before peak season if your stock position is uncertain.

8. Returns and customer service

US customers expect easy returns and fast replies. Many marketplaces set standard return windows (often around 30 days, sometimes extended for the holidays) and track response times and order defect rates. Decide in advance how you will handle returns: restock, liquidate or dispose. Build expected return costs into your pricing, and make sure someone can answer messages in English within the platform's required timeframe.

出海美国前必须准备好的 8 件事

Atronia Innovations · 2026-09-26 · 约 5 分钟阅读

很多工厂带着好产品、好价格进入美国市场，却卡在了 Listing 被下架、货物被海关扣留、品牌被跟卖这些问题上。其实这些坑大多可以提前避开。下面是首次在美国上线前，建议提前准备好的 8 件事。

不同品类、平台和州的要求各不相同，政策也会随时调整。本文仅作为入门清单，具体要求请以官方信息为准，并咨询专业人士（报关行、商标律师、税务顾问等）。

1. 主体与税务基础

是否需要注册美国公司，取决于你在哪个平台、以什么方式销售。有些平台接受中国主体直接入驻；有些平台或其中的部分项目，可能要求美国主体、美国税号或美国银行账户。注册前请先查看各平台最新的卖家入驻要求。

关于销售税：美国大多数征收销售税的州都有“市场促进者”（Marketplace Facilitator）法规，一般由平台负责代收代缴通过平台成交订单的销售税。但如果通过独立站销售，情况不同，达到某些州的销售门槛后可能产生申报义务。所得税和公司架构又是另一回事。本文不构成税务建议，做决定前请咨询熟悉跨境业务的税务专业人士。

2. 美国商标

已注册或申请中的美国商标可以保护你的品牌名称，也往往是加入亚马逊品牌备案（Brand Registry）以及其他平台类似品牌项目的关键。这些项目通常能解锁 A+ 等增强内容、品牌旗舰店、对 Listing 信息更强的控制权，以及投诉侵权的工具。具体资格要求请以各平台最新规则为准。

两点实操建议：尽早申请，因为 USPTO 审查往往需要好几个月；另外，美国境外申请人一般必须委托具有美国执业资格的律师代理。印包装之前，先做商标近似查询。

3. 产品合规与认证

美国的合规要求按品类区分，平台也可能在允许销售前要求你提交相关文件。以下是几个常见例子（并非完整清单）：

  • 电子产品：会发射射频的设备，通常需要 FCC 设备认证。
  • 儿童产品：面向 12 岁及以下儿童的产品，一般需要由 CPSC 认可的第三方实验室检测，并出具儿童产品证书（CPC）。
  • FDA 监管产品：食品、化妆品、医疗器械及部分相关产品，有各自的注册、标签或备案要求。

其他品类还可能涉及 UL 类安全标准、加州 65 号提案（Prop 65）警示等州级规定，或受限类目审核。请向检测机构或合规顾问确认你所在品类的具体要求。

4. 产品责任险

在美国，一旦产品造成人身伤害或财产损失，卖家可能面临高额索赔。部分平台要求卖家在销售额达到一定水平后购买产品责任险，大型零售商通常也会要求提供保单。即使暂时不是强制要求，也建议尽早询价，把保险成本算进利润模型。具体保额和“附加被保险人”要求，请查看平台最新政策。

5. 进口清关与物流

每一票货都需要一个进口商（Importer of Record，IOR），负责海关合规、正确归类和缴纳关税。平台一般不会充当你的 IOR，所以要提前确定由谁来承担。持牌报关行可以帮你申报，并在 HS 编码、海关保证金（Customs Bond）以及当前税率方面给出建议——近几年关税变化频繁，务必以最新税率为准。

清关之后，常见的履约方式有：

  • FBA（亚马逊物流）或 WFS（沃尔玛履约服务）：由平台负责仓储、拣货、打包和配送。
  • 美国本土 3PL 海外仓：一套库存同时供应多个渠道，包括独立站。
  • 自发货 + 折扣面单：适合小批量或大件商品。可以用我们的运费计算器比价，也可以参考如何降低美国运费。

6. Listing 内容与本地化

美国消费者比价很快，你的 Listing 要让人感觉像本土品牌：

  • 使用美制单位（英寸、磅、液量盎司、华氏度），涉及电器时注明美国插头和电压规格。
  • 文案要用地道的美式英语：标题和五点描述突出卖点和使用场景，避免机翻痕迹。
  • 遵守各平台的图片规范（例如主图白底等要求），并补充贴近美国家庭生活场景的场景图。
  • 调研美国买家真正在搜的关键词，它们往往和你内部的产品叫法不一样。

7. 广告预算与上新计划

新品很少能靠自然流量起量。要提前规划上新预算，包括站内广告、样品，以及符合平台规则的早期评论获取项目。前几周算法还在学习、你也在测词，广告花费通常会偏高；建议设定目标 ACoS 或 TACoS，以便判断何时加码、何时收缩。上新节奏要配合库存到仓时间，如果备货没把握，尽量不要赶在旺季前夕仓促上线。

8. 退货与客服

美国消费者习惯了便捷退货和快速回复。很多平台都有标准退货期（通常在 30 天左右，节假日期间有时会延长），并考核回复时效和订单缺陷率等指标。提前想好退货怎么处理：重新上架、清货还是销毁；把预估退货成本计入定价；并确保有人能在平台规定时间内用英文回复买家消息。
