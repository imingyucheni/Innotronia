CPSC eFiling: Product Certificates for US Importers and Sellers

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-11 · 6 min read

If you import consumer products into the US, product safety paperwork is no longer something you keep in a drawer until someone asks. Since July 8, 2026, the certificate that shows your product meets US safety rules must be filed electronically with customs for most regulated imports. This guide explains which certificate you need, what data goes into the filing, and how factories and brands can get ready.

The rule in brief

On January 8, 2025, the US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) published its final rule on Certificates of Compliance in the Federal Register (90 FR 1800). Made in consultation with US Customs and Border Protection (CBP), it updates CPSC's certificate rule (16 CFR Part 1110) and requires electronic filing ("eFiling") of certificate data with CBP when regulated products are imported.

Which certificate do you need?

Not every product needs a certificate; it depends on whether CPSC rules, bans or standards apply to it. If they do, there are two types:

  • Children's Product Certificate (CPC) for products designed or intended mainly for children 12 and under. Certification must be based on testing by a CPSC-accepted third-party lab.
  • General Certificate of Conformity (GCC) for general-use products covered by a CPSC rule (for example, certain mattresses, bicycle helmets or products with specific flammability rules). Certification must be based on a test of each product or a reasonable testing program; third-party testing is not required.

If you are unsure whether your product is regulated, start with CPSC's eFiling resources, including the "Does eFiling apply to me?" page, or ask a testing lab or customs broker.

What goes into the filing

According to law firm GDLSK (May 19, 2026), the certificate data filed with CBP includes:

  • Product identification, with at least one unique identifier (such as GTIN, model number, SKU or UPC) and a description that matches the product to the certificate
  • Each CPSC rule the product is certified to
  • Contact details of the certifier and the person who keeps the test records
  • Date and place of manufacture
  • Date and place of testing
  • An attestation that the product complies

There are two ways to file through CBP's ACE system. With the full message set, all certificate data is sent with each entry. With the reference message set, the importer stores certificates in CPSC's Product Registry and sends only a reference ID with each entry, which is easier for products imported repeatedly.

Who does what

The importer of record is responsible for the certificate and the filing; in practice the customs broker usually transmits the data. That means factories that ship on DDP terms or act as importer for their own US sales carry this responsibility themselves. Brands that buy from factories need the factory's test reports and production data early enough to give the broker before the goods arrive.

Missing or wrong certificate data can lead to delays in releasing your shipment and more inspections at the port, GDLSK notes. For marketplace sellers, a container held in port during Q4 can mean stock-outs at the worst time.

Step-by-step readiness checklist

  1. List your SKUs and mark which are children's products, which are general-use products covered by a CPSC rule, and which are not regulated.
  2. Collect test reports from a CPSC-accepted lab for children's products, and test or testing-program records for general-use products.
  3. Create one certificate per product (CPC or GCC) with all required data, including manufacture date and place.
  4. Decide how to file: full message set or reference IDs through the CPSC Product Registry.
  5. Brief your customs broker and send certificate data before each shipment leaves.
  6. Update certificates when you change factory, materials or production lot information.
  7. Keep records for at least five years, and keep copies ready for marketplace compliance requests.

美国 CPSC 证书电子申报（eFiling）：进口商与卖家指南

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-11 · 约 6 分钟阅读

向美国进口消费品，产品安全证书已经不再是“放在抽屉里、有人问再拿出来”的文件了。自 2026 年 7 月 8 日起，大多数受监管产品进口时，证明产品符合美国安全法规的证书必须以电子方式向海关申报。本文讲清楚你需要哪种证书、申报包含哪些数据，以及工厂和品牌该如何准备。

规则要点

2025 年 1 月 8 日，美国消费品安全委员会（CPSC）在《联邦公报》（90 FR 1800）发布了《合规证书》最终规则。该规则与美国海关与边境保护局（CBP）协商制定，更新了 CPSC 的证书规定（16 CFR Part 1110），要求受监管产品进口时向 CBP 电子申报证书数据（eFiling）。

你需要哪种证书？

并非所有产品都需要证书，取决于该产品是否受 CPSC 的法规、禁令或标准约束。若受约束，证书分两类：

  • 儿童产品证书（CPC）：适用于主要为 12 岁及以下儿童设计或使用的产品，必须基于 CPSC 认可的第三方实验室的检测结果。
  • 一般符合性证书（GCC）：适用于受 CPSC 法规约束的普通消费品（例如部分床垫、自行车头盔或有特定阻燃要求的产品），需基于对每个产品的检测或合理的检测计划，不强制第三方检测。

不确定产品是否受监管，可先查看 CPSC 的eFiling 资料页（包括“eFiling 是否适用于我”页面），或咨询检测机构、报关行。

申报包含哪些数据

据律所 GDLSK（2026 年 5 月 19 日）介绍，向 CBP 申报的证书数据包括：

  • 产品识别信息：至少一个唯一标识（如 GTIN、型号、SKU 或 UPC），以及能把产品与证书对应起来的描述
  • 产品所认证的每一项 CPSC 法规
  • 认证方及检测记录保管人的联系方式
  • 生产日期和地点
  • 检测日期和地点
  • 产品合规声明

通过 CBP 的 ACE 系统有两种申报方式：完整报文，每票报关都提交全部证书数据；引用报文，进口商先把证书存入 CPSC 产品登记系统（Product Registry），每票只传一个引用编号，更适合反复进口的同款产品。

谁负责什么

证书和申报的责任在进口商（importer of record），实际通常由报关行传输数据。这意味着按 DDP 条款发货、或自己作为进口商做美国销售的工厂，需要自己承担这项责任。向工厂采购的品牌，则要提前拿到工厂的检测报告和生产信息，赶在货到之前交给报关行。

GDLSK 提醒，证书数据缺失或错误可能导致清关延误、港口查验增加。对平台卖家来说，Q4 一个柜子卡在港口，就可能在最关键的时候断货。

分步准备清单

  1. 梳理 SKU：标出哪些是儿童产品、哪些是受 CPSC 法规约束的普通产品、哪些不受监管。
  2. 收集检测报告：儿童产品需 CPSC 认可实验室的报告；普通产品需检测或检测计划记录。
  3. 每个产品出一份证书（CPC 或 GCC），填齐所有数据，包括生产日期和地点。
  4. 确定申报方式：完整报文，或通过 CPSC 产品登记系统使用引用编号。
  5. 与报关行对接：每票货出运前把证书数据发给报关行。
  6. 及时更新证书：更换工厂、材料或批次信息时同步更新。
  7. 保存记录至少 5 年，并备好副本以应对平台的合规审核。
