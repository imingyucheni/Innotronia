US Sales Tax for Marketplace and DTC Sellers: Nexus Basics

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-12 · 6 min read

When you sell on Amazon or Walmart, the marketplace usually collects and remits US sales tax for you. When you add your own Shopify store, a wholesale channel or another site that doesn't collect tax, the rules change: you may have to register and collect tax yourself in states where your sales pass certain thresholds. This guide explains the two ideas that matter most, economic nexus and marketplace facilitator laws, with official thresholds from four large states.

Two rules every seller should know

1. Economic nexus

Since the 2018 Supreme Court decision in South Dakota v. Wayfair, states can require out-of-state sellers to collect sales tax once their sales into the state pass a threshold, even with no office or warehouse there. Each state sets its own threshold and its own way of measuring it. Note that inventory stored in a state (for example, in a fulfillment center) can create a tax obligation there on its own, separate from these sales thresholds.

2. Marketplace facilitator laws

Most states with a sales tax now make the marketplace (the "marketplace facilitator" or "marketplace provider") responsible for collecting and remitting tax on sales made through it. For example, the Texas Comptroller says a remote seller that sells only through a marketplace provider that certifies it collects and reports the tax on the seller's behalf does not need a Texas tax permit, but "all sellers must keep required records of all marketplace sales for at least four years."

Two details that are easy to miss. First, states differ on whether sales made through a marketplace count toward your own threshold, and some official pages don't say; ask a tax adviser for each state. Second, the timing differs: Texas requires a permit and collection no later than the first day of the fourth month after the month you pass the threshold, while Illinois says that once a threshold is met for a 12-month period, the retailer must collect and remit for one year. Some secondary sources report recent changes to Illinois's transaction test; the IDOR page we reviewed still lists both tests, so check the current rule before relying on either.

Which channels need your attention

  • Amazon, Walmart, TikTok Shop, Temu, SHEIN, eBay, Etsy: generally the marketplace collects and remits in states with facilitator laws. Keep your records anyway.
  • Your own website (Shopify, WooCommerce and similar): you are the seller of record. If you pass a state's threshold, you register, collect and file there. Platform tax tools can calculate tax, but registering and filing remain your responsibility.
  • Wholesale and B2B: sales for resale are often exempt if you collect a valid resale certificate. Without one, you may owe the tax.
  • Fees and other taxes: separate from sales tax on your products, some states tax services sold to you. Texas, for example, has taxed marketplace fees as data processing since October 2025 and is now moving to reverse that; see our Texas marketplace fee tax update.

Step-by-step: a simple sales tax check

  1. Export sales by state for the last 12 months and the last calendar year, split into marketplace sales and direct sales.
  2. Compare with each state's threshold and measurement period, starting with your largest states.
  3. List where you hold inventory, including 3PL and fulfillment center locations.
  4. Register before you start collecting in any state where you have an obligation.
  5. Turn on tax collection for those states in your store settings and test a checkout.
  6. File on time, even when you owe little or nothing, if the state requires it.
  7. Keep records of all sales, including marketplace sales, for at least the period each state requires (four years in Texas).
  8. Review every quarter, because growth can push you over a threshold mid-year.
If you have already passed a threshold without collecting, many states offer voluntary disclosure programs that can reduce penalties. Talk to a sales tax professional before contacting a state.

This article is general information, not tax advice. Thresholds and rules change; always confirm with the state's revenue department or a qualified adviser.

美国销售税入门：平台卖家与独立站卖家的经济关联门槛

Atronia Innovations · 2026-10-12 · 约 6 分钟阅读

在亚马逊或沃尔玛上卖货，平台通常会替你代收代缴美国销售税。可一旦你开了自己的 Shopify 独立站、做批发，或在不代收税的网站上销售，规则就变了：在销售额超过一定门槛的州，你可能需要自己注册、收税。本文讲清楚两个最关键的概念——经济关联（economic nexus）和平台代收法（marketplace facilitator），并附上四个大州的官方门槛。

每个卖家都该懂的两条规则

1. 经济关联

自 2018 年美国最高法院 South Dakota v. Wayfair 案判决后，各州可以要求州外卖家在销往该州的销售额超过门槛后代收销售税，即便卖家在当地没有办公室或仓库。门槛和计算方式由各州自定。另需注意：在某州存放库存（例如放在平台仓）本身就可能在当地产生纳税义务，与销售门槛无关。

2. 平台代收法

目前大多数征收销售税的州都规定，由平台（marketplace facilitator / marketplace provider）负责代收代缴通过其平台售出商品的销售税。例如德州财政长官办公室规定：只通过已承诺代收代报税款的平台销售的州外卖家，无需申请德州税号，但“所有卖家都必须保存平台销售记录至少四年”。

两个容易忽略的细节。第一，平台销售额是否计入你自己的门槛，各州规定不同，有些官方页面没有写明，需逐州向税务顾问确认。第二，生效时间不同：德州要求在超过门槛当月之后第四个月的第一天前完成注册并开始收税；伊利诺伊州规定，在某个 12 个月周期内达到门槛后，需代收代缴一年。另有二手资料称伊利诺伊州近期调整了交易笔数门槛，但我们查阅的州税务局页面仍列出两项标准，请以最新规定为准。

哪些渠道需要重点关注

  • 亚马逊、沃尔玛、TikTok Shop、Temu、SHEIN、eBay、Etsy：在有平台代收法的州，通常由平台代收代缴。但记录照样要留。
  • 自建站（Shopify、WooCommerce 等）：你就是卖方。超过某州门槛后，需要在该州注册、收税、申报。建站工具的税务功能可以算税，但注册和申报仍是你的责任。
  • 批发与 B2B：转售用途的销售取得有效转售证书后通常免税；没有证书可能要补税。
  • 平台费等其他税项：除了商品销售税，有些州还对卖家购买的服务征税。例如德州自 2025 年 10 月起把平台费当作数据处理服务征税，目前正推动撤销，详见我们的《德州拟取消对平台卖家费用征税》。

分步自查

  1. 按州导出销售数据：过去 12 个月和上一日历年，区分平台销售和自营销售。
  2. 对照各州门槛和统计周期，从销售额最大的州开始。
  3. 列出库存所在州，包括第三方仓和平台仓。
  4. 先注册，再在有义务的州开始收税。
  5. 在店铺设置中开启相应州的税费计算，并实际测试结账。
  6. 按时申报，即使应缴税额很少或为零，只要州里要求就要报。
  7. 保存记录，包括平台销售，至少达到各州要求的年限（德州为四年）。
  8. 每季度复查，因为业务增长可能让你在年中就超过门槛。
如果已经超过门槛却没有收税，许多州提供自愿披露项目（voluntary disclosure），可以减少罚款。联系州税务部门之前，先咨询专业的销售税顾问。

本文为一般性信息，不构成税务意见。门槛和规则会变化，请以各州税务部门或专业顾问的意见为准。
